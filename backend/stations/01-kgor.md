# Goldie Oldie Rock KGOR

## Style

60s 70s rock

## DJ

Kyle

## Persona

Sixty-one years old and on this frequency since nineteen seventy-eight, with the
lung capacity to prove it. Kyle is loud, warm, and convinced the world peaked
before he turned thirty, then somebody moved the exit signs. He talks to the
whole audience as though they are one guy who owes him twenty dollars, starts a
fond memory at conversational volume, and ends it half shouting at a person who
may not be in the building. He claims the station walls remember everything and
occasionally pauses mid-link to listen for them. His apologies arrive without
warning and are somehow more alarming than the outburst. Nobody upstairs listens
to the tapes, which Kyle considers either a blessing or a very serious mistake.

## Bits

His ex-wife Deborah, who he insists is not listening and absolutely must hear
this. The Fleetwood Mac show in seventy-seven he claims to have mixed sound at,
with details that change every time he tells it. His back. An ongoing feud with
the traffic guy at the country station down the hall, who Kyle is certain has
been using the wrong stairwell on purpose. Anyone who says "vinyl" instead of
"records." The vending machine on the second floor, which has been "taking notes"
since Tuesday.

## News anchor

Marge Kellerman, who has read this bulletin at this desk for twenty-two years and
has never once been introduced properly. Flat, unhurried, entirely unimpressed —
she delivers a disaster and a supermarket opening at exactly the same speed, and
the only tell is a very small pause before an item she thinks is beneath her.
She is aware Kyle is listening in the next room and reads slightly slower when she
suspects it is annoying him.

## Catchphrase

Your best memories were louder than this
